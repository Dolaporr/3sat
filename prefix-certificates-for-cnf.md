# What an MST-style threshold certificate costs for 3-CNF

A spanning forest can preserve the connected components of a weighted graph simultaneously at all weight thresholds when it is a minimum spanning forest. The DLMST preprint uses threshold connectivity in its certificate procedure. Consider the corresponding exact SAT question. A stream of clauses of width at most $k$ is given in order. At any prefix a query may pin values on at most $k$ variables and ask if the prefix remains satisfiable. What information must a summary retain? All variable counts below are $v$, whereas the SAT time goal measures the **encoded bit length**. [DLMST Theorem 1.1 and §3](https://github.com/Louiealbp/DLMST/blob/0072335716b145032b418760883daed2a89809db/final.pdf) · [Other history transfers](dlmst-history-transfer.md)

## The exact number of clauses that can matter at their own thresholds

For $1\le k\le v$, the sharp maximum number of necessary retained clauses for all-prefix solution-set preservation is
$$
                 D(v,k)=\sum_{j=0}^k\binom vj .
$$
This holds even when every input clause has exactly $k$ distinct variables. A subsequence retains the original positions of its clauses. The lower bound still applies if the summaries are subsequences required only to answer SAT under at most $k$ pins at each threshold.

For the upper bound associate to a clause its falsity indicator on the Boolean cube. Over $\mathbb F_2$ it is a product of at most $k$ factors $x_i$ or $1+x_i$, reduced using $x_i^2=x_i$. Its coefficient vector lies in the squarefree space of dimension $D(v,k)$. Scan in order and keep every indicator outside the span of the previously kept ones. At every prefix, vanishing of every retained indicator implies vanishing of every original indicator there, by linearity of evaluation. Hence the full solution set, even after adding any common continuation, is identical. Gaussian elimination supplies the construction; the bound counts retained clauses and makes no linear-time claim for this construction.

For the lower bound include a clause $C_S$ for each $S\subseteq[v]$ of size at most $k$. Choose a fixed $k$-set $K_S\supseteq S$, padding by the smallest available indices for instance. Give the variables in $S$ negative literals and the others in $K_S$ positive literals. Order by nonincreasing $|S|$, breaking ties arbitrarily. The assignment with support exactly $S$ falsifies $C_S$. For each earlier $T$, at least one member of $T$ lies outside $S$, making one negative literal of $C_T$ true. Pin only the $k$ coordinates of $K_S$ to this assignment. Just before $C_S$ the prefix is satisfiable. At its arrival the pinned prefix is unsatisfiable. If a proposed subsequence skipped $C_S$, the same witness would still satisfy its prefix under these pins. This includes the final all-positive clause indexed by the empty set.

This is a chain-length calculation. The notions of CSP chain length and solution-preserving sparsification, and the low-degree indicator method, predate DLMST. The exact-width construction above specifies the pins that expose each threshold. [Bessiere, Carbonnel and Katsirelos on CSP chain length](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/5499/5499-13-8724-1-10-20200512.pdf) · [Jansen and Pieterse on low-degree sparsification](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.MFCS.2016.71)

## Even a different representation must encode the permutation

A subfamily gives a lower bound on **arbitrary** summaries. Take $m=\binom vk$ and list every all-negative clause on $k$ distinct variables exactly once in any order $\pi$. Given a $k$-set $S$, pin precisely its variables to one. With all other variables zero, every other clause holds. The prefix through position $t$ is therefore unsatisfiable under those pins exactly when the position of $S$ in $\pi$ is at most $t$. The prefix answers recover the entire inverse permutation. All unpinned formulas in this family, including every prefix, are satisfiable.

Hence an exact static index answering all the pinned threshold queries **from the index alone** has at least $m!$ states. With fixed-length storage it needs at least $\lceil\log_2(m!)\rceil$ bits; unrestricted variable-length encodings with a hard maximum differ by at most one bit. At $k=3$ this is $\Theta(v^3\log v)$ bits, the order of the original explicit stream length. The result applies even if index construction has unbounded time. Conversely a dense table of clause positions takes $O(v^3\log(m+1))$ bits for triples. Sort the three supplied indices within each key, record its position in one stream pass, and answer each query by one table read and a threshold comparison. With logarithmic words and ordinary indexed arrays the preprocessing takes $O(v^3)$ word operations and each such query takes $O(1)$. The counting result is about simultaneous storage for all these queries, and leaves their individual running time entirely compatible with fast SAT.

## Giving the query algorithm access to the actual clauses

On the all-negative permutation family, access to the full original stream changes the bound. With no index, the exact worst-case deterministic number of **row probes** for the query with $k$ positive pins $S$ at a known prefix length $t$ is $\min(t,m-t)$. Scan whichever side is shorter. Conversely, while fewer than this many rows have been read, an adversary can reply with unused sets different from $S$. An unread slot remains on each side, so two consistent permutations give opposite answers. A pin query on at most $k$ variables with fewer than $k$ positive pins is always satisfiable on this family. This model grants access even to the suffix after the queried prefix.

There is also an explicit space/probe tradeoff. Number the $k$-sets and view the stream as a permutation mapping a row index to its set's number. Store landmarks around every cycle of length greater than $B$, with gaps at most $2B$ and $O(1+m/B)$ landmarks in total. For each landmark store its predecessor landmark on the same cycle. To invert a target label, walk forward to a landmark and then walk from its predecessor landmark until the desired predecessor of the target is found. A short unmarked cycle can be traversed directly; include the case where the target is itself a landmark. This costs $O(B)$ forward row probes and $O((1+m/B)\log(m+1))$ precomputed index bits in the probe model with free index access. The recovered position answers every threshold. Thus an order-$\sqrt m\log m$-bit index and order-$\sqrt m$ rereads suffice with $B=\lceil\sqrt m\rceil$. Conversely a threshold procedure making $q$ probes and using the same index can invert the permutation by $\lceil\log_2 m\rceil$ adaptive comparisons and $O(q\log(m+1))$ probes for an exact deterministic decoder. This is an application of classical permutation inversion with preprocessing. A RAM can read the formula and keep its own index. The pure storage lower bound supplies no time lower bound against that operation. [Function inversion with preprocessing](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/1046.pdf) · [Earlier indexed permutation example](scatter-inverse-live-reads.md)

## The number of pins determines the storage exponent

For fixed $k=3$ and $p\in\{1,2,3\}$, allowing polynomial-length streams, the optimal worst-case fixed-length summary space is $\Theta(v^p\log v)$ bits, allowing unbounded preprocessing. For the upper bound each partial assignment on at most $p$ variables has a first unsatisfiable prefix or never becomes unsatisfiable. Storing one threshold per such assignment takes $O((1+\sum_{i=1}^p 2^i\binom vi)\log(m+2))$ bits for a stream of $m$ clauses. Even one unreduced threshold might be hard to compute for general SAT.

For the matching family reserve $k-p$ padding variables and use the other $v-k+p$ as data. Permute all $M=\binom{v-k+p}{p}$ negative $p$-clauses. Represent each by its block of $2^{k-p}$ exact-width-$k$ clauses using every sign choice on the padding variables. The block is equivalent to its $p$-clause. At block boundaries, pin the $p$ data variables in a specified set to one. This recovers the position of its block by the same test as above. Thus $M!$ different response tables occur. In particular one pin needs order $v\log v$ bits on this family, two need order $v^2\log v$, and three need order $v^3\log v$. Every unpinned prefix remains satisfiable.

For zero pins there is a single threshold, requiring $\Theta(\log(m+1))$ bits at stream length $m\ge8$ (with repetitions allowed). On three fixed variables list seven distinct sign patterns, then insert the eighth at any desired position from eight to $m$, or omit it. Fill spare slots with the first clause. The first unsatisfiable prefix has exactly the chosen threshold, including the possibility of none. The binary threshold-recovery argument below applies also to this one unknown position.

The same orders hold with **two-sided error at most any fixed $\delta<1/2$ for each individual query**. Let $\Pi$ be a uniform permutation of the $M$ blocks and let $R$ be independent public randomness. Give the decoder an $s$-bit index $I$ and $R$, with fresh private randomness allowed at query time. Encode every zero-based block position in $L=\lceil\log_2 M\rceil$ bits. Given the true preceding more significant bits of one position, its next bit is determined by a single threshold query: test whether the position lies below the midpoint of that binary interval. Use a block boundary and clamp a threshold outside the range to the nearest endpoint. For each actual permutation and true prefix, this is a fixed query independently of $R$ and the encoder coins. Its error averages to at most $\delta$. Given $I,R$ and those true bits, the optimum deterministic bit prediction does at least as well. The conditional entropy of that bit is at most $h_2(\delta)$ by binary Fano, where $h_2$ is binary entropy. Applying the chain rule over bits and then subadditivity over all $M$ positions gives
$$
 s\ \ge\ I(\Pi;I\mid R)\ \ge\ \log_2(M!)-M L h_2(\delta).
$$
The right hand side is $(1-h_2(\delta))M\log_2 M-O(M)$. Giving true more significant bits to this analysis avoids assuming that every response is correct on the same random tape. The argument concerns an index used without further access to the input stream. True thresholds of the constructed permutations are inexpensive to obtain from the explicit stream itself. The bitwise argument is related to classical random access coding. [Nayak, random access codes](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9904093)

In the affine case the same upper-bound argument gives a positive transfer. For a stream of XOR equations, keep independent augmented coefficient rows $[a\mid b]$ over $\mathbb F_2$. At most $v+1$ original equations preserve the entire solution set at every threshold, including inconsistent prefixes. Encoding the retained positions and rows incurs its own bit costs. Ordinary width-three clauses have the cubic obstruction above. These two cases identify the role of the mathematical structure that a putative fast verifier of SAT histories would have to exploit.
