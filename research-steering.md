# Steering the SAT lower-bound project

**24 November 2026.** An unconditional exclusion of deterministic linear time for ordinary explicit SAT on a logarithmic-word RAM with unrestricted workspace is the first milestone. The full objective requires a quadratic- or cubic-time exclusion. The project has no proof of any of those exclusions.

## What counts as progress on the lower bound

A results entry should name an unconditional theorem that the proposed fast solver would contradict, specify the machines and input encoding quantified over, and prove an implication for **every** solver in its stated scope. It should give the simulation costs where they enter the contradiction. Put restricted models and remaining premises in the statement. Record a finite experiment, conditional reformulation or failed proof as research history; none counts as an unrestricted time lower bound. Scope a Lean claim to the definitions and lemmas it actually checks.

The earlier fixed-program test is equivalent to the original question. Its equivalence proof identifies a condition to investigate, while supplying no independent proof of that condition. The oracle clock construction gives linear time in a model with a specially constructed recursive oracle and oracle gates. A proof that would remain valid for that oracle model has a problem. Applying the construction to ordinary explicitly encoded CNFs without those oracle gates would require an additional argument. ## Results from the five proposed directions

1. **Locally decodable solver memory** was the first priority. The checkpoint proof now takes a shared description of the actual solver's memory, with the input available to the decoder at every query. It forces $\Omega(n/\log^4 n)$ bits for a linear solver and a fixed constant-time decoder on a family of unsatisfiable formulas. Even a decoder required to answer only actual incoming reads needs $\Omega(n/\log^7 n)$ bits on a second family with a finer grid. The proofs allow shared data across all checkpoints. The bounded diagonal is sensitive to the time of an individual honest certificate. How short a representation is forced by linear running time alone remains unresolved. [Proof and decoder interfaces](solver-snapshot-descriptions.md) · [Cross-block producer count](solver-cross-block-writes.md)
2. **Alternation trading at logarithmic precision.** Allowing a different previously derived simulation at each checkpoint level excludes $b=6.15484525$ at linear time. The same profile rule cannot reach a contradiction at $b=6.15475$ or smaller. The upper calculation uses 2,401 finite transformations, and an optimistic lower envelope survives all 718 relevant ordered tuples. Methods with a different space representation or different input charges remain possible. [Mixed calculation and rule scope](mixed-checkpoint-profiles.md) · [Earlier finite proof](log-factor-trades.md) · [Williams](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/automated-lbs.pdf) · [Buss and Williams](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/npProofLimits/samCCC-cr.pdf)
3. **Fixed-dimensional tapes.** Storing a cube at every visit gives the proposed $1/(2d)$ saving in the logarithmic exponent. Grouping visits before taking a snapshot gives $1/2$ for every fixed $d$. The proof uses the published deterministic circuit-CSP bound, with finite-alphabet local head movements. [Derivation](multidimensional-tape-simulation.md) · [Strassle and Subercaseaux, Fact 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11832v1)
4. **Two-dimensional segregators.** A row sweep followed by a column sweep contracts to a complete bipartite graph at the row/column time scale. The example nevertheless has small segregators. A spatial partition proves a sublinear bound for all one-head cell graphs; further independent heads break its confinement argument. The subsequent two-plane construction gives actual block graphs with no sublinear segregators and accounts for every incidental head visit. This excludes a direct extension of the graph lemma to all such machines. [Two-plane proof](two-plane-directed-grates.md) · [Robust terminal sets](two-tape-robust-terminals.md) [Sweep and partition proof](two-dimensional-segregators.md) · [PPST's precise graph condition](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/34.pdf)
5. **Permanent on the cube at individual degree two.** The squared-row example has zero formal set-multilinear part. A truncated-square-root substitution still extracts the formal permanent efficiently in odd characteristic or characteristic zero. This extends to per-variable powers with boundedly many exceptions per monomial. An explicit diagonal term destroys that hypothesis while vanishing on the cube. Unrestricted degree-two representatives remain open here. [Extraction and counterexample](degree-two-permanent.md) · [Forbes, Kumar and Saptharishi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.04207)

A sole hard family can cover a sound effectively enumerated collection of solvers, while a time-preserving shortcut escapes that family. The unrestricted time exclusions require an independent upper bound on the matching solver's history, or a different contradiction. ## A resource bound without a prescribed time or space cap

Summing one plus the current nonzero-word count over each prehalt instruction gives a separate worst-case lower bound on UNSAT. Sparse checkpoints chosen inside disjoint windows transform a complementary simulation exponent by $e\mapsto cde/(e+d)$, where $c$ bounds the target's cumulative exponent and $d$ bounds the time exponent of a reference recognizer. This excludes every pure cumulative exponent below the golden ratio. At linear reference time a direct calculation gives $\Omega(n^2/\log^{10} n)$ cumulative word-steps for any deterministic target on a family of unsatisfiable inputs. A linear-time RAM can accrue quadratic cumulative cost, leaving the elapsed-time problem unresolved. ## From snapshots to reads that actually occur

A shifted time grid charges a read at time $r$ to its preceding same-address touch $p$. Its chance of crossing a block boundary is at most $\min(1,(r-p)/w)$ for block length $w$. A predecessor record is checked by finding an intervening event as an ordinary existential defect. The positive-run calculation charges the interval when the read occurs, so the solver does not need to predict which words will still be live. A value-only predictor can omit a timestamp at a correctly predicted incoming read; checking for a wrong value then uses another nonacceptance call. This gives an exact-rational $1.3622$ unconditional exponent for the sum of the gaps incurred by wrong read values, plus elapsed steps. ## Deriving the initial space band

For the explicit packed encoding and word operations in the proof, a deterministic solver taking $O(n\log^a n)$ time cannot also use $O(n/\log^{2a+7} n)$ workspace bits, for each fixed nonnegative integer $a$. At $a=0$ the exponent is **7**. The contradiction is with a nondeterministic diagonal language's exclusion from smaller conondeterministic time. The notes derive 9 and 8 first, then 7 by reducing the cost of the read-only checkpoint input in the last SAT call. ## Where the hierarchy proof stops

In the direct simulation, a configuration with $S$ live words costs $\Theta(S)$ words to specify. With $P$ candidate segments, the input to the error checker contains $\Theta(PS)$ words and its replay takes at least the simulated segment's allotted time. Nesting improves the balance while the snapshots fit. It gives no saving when even a single snapshot can cost the whole time budget. For a linear-time RAM the elementary cap is $O(n)$ touched words, or $O(n\log n)$ bits. That is $b=-1$ in the notation of the band. A candidate unrestricted speedup must account for reconstruction and checking of memory read by a later segment when earlier segments wrote to arbitrary addresses. A new small-space *simulation* would have to retain the time bound required by the hierarchy argument.

PPST separated deterministic and nondeterministic linear time on multitape Turing machines. Williams proved a time-to-space simulation on tapes. Strassle and Subercaseaux give a deterministic simulation of nondeterministic tape time. These are model- and resource-specific statements. There is also a genuine pure-space consequence for RAMs: Li and Tell record Williams's observation that quadratic simulation by one tape followed by a two-fifths-power space simulation gives about $t^{4/5}$ space up to logarithms. The derivation supplies no corresponding near-linear time bound. [PPST](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/34.pdf) · [Williams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17779) · [Strassle and Subercaseaux](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11832) · [Li and Tell, §1 footnote 1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/233/download/)

The decoded-snapshot and producer proofs give such speedups for two classes of random-access executions that can touch large memory. Extending their coverage to every fast solver remains the prerequisite for this route. A proof must certify that every imported word has the value of its last write and pay for this certification in the saved clock. A construction for a strict subclass should identify how an unrestricted solver could escape it. The hierarchy is one route to a contradiction; the gate also admits a different unconditional theorem with a proved transfer.

Ordinary SAT in the stated explicit encoding can guess an assignment and check the formula in linear word time. Excluding a deterministic linear-time solver would therefore also separate nondeterministic from deterministic linear time on this RAM. Quasilinear completeness in the opposite direction loses logarithmic factors. A lower bound for an arithmetic circuit or for permanent-graph decision trees likewise needs a separate, costed transfer to Boolean SAT. [SAT transfer and its logarithmic losses](https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/tst.pdf) · [Permanent-graph analysis](pr249-grid.md)

The additive query measure led to a different verification argument. Guess a single segment's adaptive read log and use a separate nonacceptance test for an incorrect decoded value. The cumulative sum of shortest description length plus query time now excludes every exponent below $1.3760858894\ldots$ unconditionally. Under linear SAT there is an interval of $\Omega(n/\log^8 n)$ times with additive query cost $\Omega(n/\log^{12} n)$ at every time. The same approach with an intervening-write test forces hard predictions even when only imminent incoming reads matter. A matching shortcut for arbitrary native computations is missing. [Segment audit](first-moment-snapshot-audits.md) · [Improved cumulative exponent](first-moment-heavy-bootstrap.md) · [Imminent incoming reads](live-segment-snapshot-queries.md)

A last-writer shortcut now faces a simpler test. Under linear SAT a fixed unary workload can copy data into fresh words in increasing order and read them back in that order. There is a long interval of starts on which the incoming values are hard even when their true writing times are supplied. The generation phase uses a hypothesized SAT solver; the final verdict of the workload is constant. Thus a universal prediction argument would have to use more than a simple input and a known producer schedule. A separate unrestricted-space tradeoff rules out $O(n^{1.0001})$ for rejection time multiplied by the largest clique-minor order (with ones added). Arbitrary RAMs can realize large minors in linear time. Under linear SAT a short policy still leaves a $\sqrt n/\operatorname{polylog} n$ minor on some rejecting runs. The permutation construction gives a limit on a possible shortcut. A fixed constant-verdict program can force a minor of this size even when the predictor sees the true source time and all input bits are explicitly present.
