# Steering the SAT lower-bound project

**25 November 2026.** An unconditional exclusion of deterministic linear time for ordinary explicit SAT on a logarithmic-word RAM with unrestricted workspace is the first milestone. The full objective requires a quadratic- or cubic-time exclusion. The project has no proof of any of those exclusions.

## Current working milestone

Use the native unary-clock language to target $\mathrm{NTIME}_{\rm RAM}[n]\ne\mathrm{DTIME}_{\rm RAM}[n]$. Its bounded-acceptance verifier takes linear time on the stated word RAM. On this complete problem the memory lower bound reaches $\Omega(n)$ bits in occupied nonzero words on inputs of each verdict at every sufficiently large length. The unrestricted allowance is $O(n\log n)$ bits. The explicit SAT objectives above retain the encoded formula length as their parameter. [Native theorem and proof](native-unary-clock-band.md)

The existing snapshot transfer excludes the strict exponent region $e+d<1$ at linear solver time. Storing or replaying a state attains its frontier on the inverse-permutation family. Freeze that tradeoff as a search direction unless a new argument uses a property of SAT missing from that family. The next diagonal test charges the choice bits actually carried across alternation switches. It suffices to speed up the diagonal correctly on arbitrarily long extensions of each fixed opponent-program prefix. For linear SAT the encoded lengths of the nested calls impose additional logarithmic factors. A recursive Boolean oracle, even with its canonical multilinear extension available to the source machine, makes native alternating linear time deterministic in the specified oracle model. A proof transferring to that model cannot give the separation. ## Can a short proof identify either of two losing ranks?

Fix a sound uniform verifier of a claim that rank $e$ loses at the current stage. Two programs race candidate SAT queries for proofs of their respective losses and signal on a positive answer for the selected rank. Neither can ever win. Under a fast SAT hypothesis neither admits a timely proof at any late stage. One sufficient route to an unrestricted separation is a short proof for even one of those two true losses infinitely often, for a verifier that covers the pairs formed against itself. A fixed generator also removes dependence on an unknown SAT solver. The original final-register tableau has an unconditional $\Omega(M)$ minimum-core bound at every losing pin, so the proposed $E_l+1$ small-core test fails there. Separate trial modules can certify old retirements cheaply, but their core cost for any as-yet-unretired loser is at least a constant times that rank's trial deadline. In particular, assigning both color copies to a shared prefix computation cannot defeat the short-window intervention. A word-residue analysis already yields three-clause losses on infinitely many explicit never-retired programs. The missing coverage is for the pair constructed against the chosen verifier. [Value-sensitive clauses and their limit](semantic-silent-trial-cores.md) Sparse multilinear Nullstellensatz and polynomial calculus can be checked directly on other locally indexed encodings at nearly the cost of their monomial count. The partial-history checker takes $O(j(a_j+1)4^{a_j})$ word operations on whole-column truth tables by keeping the reachable retired sets; $a_j=O(\log\log j)$. Each sound certificate has to justify the exclusions it uses. Even with almost the whole genuine past supplied, any one verifier for unsuccessful trial reports encounters an unbounded number of actual silent trials with no power-saving certificates at each late scale under the hypothesized SAT clock. Sparse exception counts give small nonuniform default descriptions and total search windows with a known rare award. They do not give a uniformly cheap certificate locating it. ## Testing smaller ordinary SAT encodings

Testing a gate on spans of monomial evaluation vectors extends the aligned checker to degree $o(\log M)$ at subpolynomial overhead. A variable-width bit-disjointness test, evaluated by a single native program, gives a sensitive load column costing $M^{1-o(1)}$ terms in this extended model. The required power saving must concern a sound verdict or a specific implementation. [Proof and finite tests](polynomial-history-sweeps.md)

On the native signed computation of clock $M$, one exact route to a smaller formula is to guess a rectangular description of the chronological and sorting-network bits. Its local checks use a sweep that handles both ordinary rectangles and XOR diagonals. Short descriptions on infinitely many scales would suffice at the explicit $M^{1/k}$ threshold up to logarithms. Generic program length, elapsed time and sparsity of the input do not give such descriptions. A parity phase in an implementation of the same signed bit forces a genuine read column with $\Omega(M)$ terms or errors. A proof of its zero parity could still allow a different verifier to bypass the phase. [Verifier, robust lower bound and signed embedding](geometric-history-certificates.md)

Separate hash challenges permit different false histories even when the genuine signed bit and every timestamp are fixed. Known exact-histories routing methods encounter a full-packet cost even for one unknown rotation offset. A further oracle test shows why sparsity alone cannot guarantee short nondeterministic witnesses for both signs at infinitely many scales: an arbitrarily slowly growing row weight can already force every late successful branch to inspect the entire promised row on a separated sequence of lengths. ## A private-coin test for the unrestricted clock

The first target for a specific protocol on the pinned $M$-step program is a family of pruned accepting trees whose exact private-coin mass exceeds soundness. To attack the quadratic or cubic clock, the explored verifier work must be $o(M^{1/k}/\log^3 M)$ on infinitely many true scales. Shared private prefixes can be simulated once, while equal public histories across branches must receive identical prover messages. A clocked nondeterministic checker then yields one ordinary SAT instance per tested cap. No protocol with the required trees is known. A different test allows many random seeds but a fixed number of messages. Approximate counting of compatible private tapes converts a two-sided statistical gap into an exact alternating computation at the verifier's packed word cost. The eventual SAT slowdown must also pay for earlier choices passed as public bits. An arbitrary binary opcode already preserves a hypothetical ordinary speedup on long packed inputs. A sparse opcode preserves one on short inputs while still defeating statistical verification of a public circuit accurate everywhere except possibly one pair. ## A superlinear SAT theorem for a restricted memory geometry

An independent check confirms both the proposed square root and the stronger iteration. A further version charges $P=\sum_a\min(r_a,w_a)$ on the extra scratch, with $r_a,w_a$ the native read and write counts at an address. On a planar tape it excludes time $O(N^{11/10})$ together with $P=O(T^{4/5})$. The proof authenticates changes or incoming reads separately at each address. At linear time it excludes $P=O(N/\log^A N)$ for some fixed $A$, even with the extra tape. A full RAM may have $P=\Theta(T)$. [Proof and scope](hybrid-scratch-read-write.md)

The hybrid machine has one head on a fixed $d$-dimensional work tape and random read-only input. If its separate random-access scratch averages at most $O(T^\omega)$ words and $0\le\omega<1/d$, the first simulation gives the proposed square-root time exponent. Iterating yields the stronger exclusion $(d+1+d\omega)c^2<c+d+1$. The chart records the scratch state at a chronological crossing and the tape state when the same tile is split across local checks. A second derivation uses exactly two quantified blocks and excludes linear time at the critical scratch exponent with a sufficiently large logarithmic saving. These proofs retain unrestricted geometric tape but impose conditions on the random-access store. [Proof and parameter range](hybrid-tape-cut-trading.md) · [Crossing descriptions and two blocks](hybrid-tape-crossing-chart.md)

For Santhanam’s Theorem 2.7, a November 2026 manuscript by J. A. Wittcop reports counterexamples to the required all-pushdown-graphs hypothesis. With a fixed number of pages, deleting at most $n/(16\lceil\log_2 n\rceil)$ vertices still leaves some vertex with a linear number of ancestors. Layers have width of order $n/\log n$. Small surviving sets expand backwards into previous layers even after the total allowed deletions, giving the required ancestor count over many layers. The target about block graphs for an appropriate machine deciding each language remains a separate question. The result and the scope of its reported Lean check are the author’s; we did not rebuild that artifact. ## What counts as progress on the lower bound

A results entry should name an unconditional theorem that the proposed fast solver would contradict, specify the machines and input encoding quantified over, and prove an implication for **every** solver in its stated scope. It should give the simulation costs where they enter the contradiction. Put restricted models and remaining premises in the statement. Record a finite experiment, conditional reformulation or failed proof as research history; none counts as an unrestricted time lower bound. Scope a Lean claim to the definitions and lemmas it actually checks.

The earlier fixed-program test is equivalent to the original question. Its equivalence proof identifies a condition to investigate, while supplying no independent proof of that condition. The oracle clock construction gives linear time in a model with a specially constructed recursive oracle and oracle gates. A proof that would remain valid for that oracle model has a problem. Applying the construction to ordinary explicitly encoded CNFs without those oracle gates would require an additional argument. [Fixed-program test](fixed-program-parity.md) · [Exact oracle model](relativized-clock.md)

## Results from the five proposed directions

1. **Locally decodable solver memory** was the first priority. The checkpoint proof now takes a shared description of the actual solver's memory, with the input available to the decoder at every query. It forces $\Omega(n/\log^4 n)$ bits for a linear solver and a fixed constant-time decoder on a family of unsatisfiable formulas. Even a decoder required to answer only actual incoming reads needs $\Omega(n/\log^7 n)$ bits on a second family with a finer grid. The proofs allow shared data across all checkpoints. The bounded diagonal is sensitive to the time of an individual honest certificate. How short a representation is forced by linear running time alone remains unresolved. [Proof and decoder interfaces](solver-snapshot-descriptions.md) · [Cross-block producer count](solver-cross-block-writes.md)
2. **Alternation trading at logarithmic precision.** Allowing a different previously derived simulation at each checkpoint level excludes $b=6.15484525$ at linear time. The same profile rule cannot reach a contradiction at $b=6.15475$ or smaller. The upper calculation uses 2,401 finite transformations, and an optimistic lower envelope survives all 718 relevant ordered tuples. Methods with a different space representation or different input charges remain possible. [Mixed calculation and rule scope](mixed-checkpoint-profiles.md) · [Earlier finite proof](log-factor-trades.md) · [Williams](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/automated-lbs.pdf) · [Buss and Williams](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/ResearchWeb/npProofLimits/samCCC-cr.pdf)
3. **Fixed-dimensional tapes.** Storing a cube at every visit gives the proposed $1/(2d)$ saving in the logarithmic exponent. Grouping visits before taking a snapshot gives $1/2$ for every fixed $d$. The proof uses the published deterministic circuit-CSP bound, with finite-alphabet local head movements. [Derivation](multidimensional-tape-simulation.md) · [Strassle and Subercaseaux, Fact 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11832v1)
4. **Two-dimensional segregators.** A row sweep followed by a column sweep contracts to a complete bipartite graph at the row/column time scale. The example nevertheless has small segregators. A spatial partition proves a sublinear bound for all one-head cell graphs; further independent heads break its confinement argument. The subsequent two-plane construction gives actual block graphs with no sublinear segregators and accounts for every incidental head visit. This excludes a direct extension of the graph lemma to all such machines. [Two-plane proof](two-plane-directed-grates.md) · [Robust terminal sets](two-tape-robust-terminals.md) [Sweep and partition proof](two-dimensional-segregators.md) · [PPST's precise graph condition](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/34.pdf)
5. **Permanent on the cube at individual degree two.** The squared-row example has zero formal set-multilinear part. A truncated-square-root substitution still extracts the formal permanent efficiently in odd characteristic or characteristic zero. This extends to per-variable powers with boundedly many exceptions per monomial. An explicit diagonal term destroys that hypothesis while vanishing on the cube. Unrestricted degree-two representatives remain open here. [Extraction and counterexample](degree-two-permanent.md) · [Forbes, Kumar and Saptharishi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.04207)

For quadratic and cubic time, checking a circuit for just the register history avoids a list of controllers at polynomially many checkpoints. Finding a wrong read in an arbitrary succinct event stream is complete for $\Sigma_2^P$ even on one bit. The two charged SAT calls yield the stated circuit lower bounds on genuine rejecting register streams. A sole hard family can cover a sound effectively enumerated collection of solvers, while a time-preserving shortcut escapes that family. The unrestricted time exclusions require an independent upper bound on the matching solver's history, or a different contradiction. [Register bounds](chronological-solver-registers.md) · [Memory defects and solver quantifiers](succinct-memory-defects.md)

The invariant search now reaches proof formats that do not specify the unique memory at each step. An adaptive checker can challenge one outgoing row and consult selected old rows on an arbitrary memory. A separate algebraic checker can cancel occurrences in dense affine equations without consulting that memory. The favorable cases differ even on one identity step. ## Where the hierarchy proof stops

In the direct simulation, a configuration with $S$ live words costs $\Theta(S)$ words to specify. With $P$ candidate segments, the input to the error checker contains $\Theta(PS)$ words and its replay takes at least the simulated segment's allotted time. Nesting improves the balance while the snapshots fit. It gives no saving when even a single snapshot can cost the whole time budget. For a linear-time RAM the elementary cap is $O(n)$ touched words, or $O(n\log n)$ bits. That is $b=-1$ in the notation of the band. A candidate unrestricted speedup must account for reconstruction and checking of memory read by a later segment when earlier segments wrote to arbitrary addresses. A new small-space *simulation* would have to retain the time bound required by the hierarchy argument.

PPST separated deterministic and nondeterministic linear time on multitape Turing machines. Williams proved a time-to-space simulation on tapes. Strassle and Subercaseaux give a deterministic simulation of nondeterministic tape time. These are model- and resource-specific statements. There is also a genuine pure-space consequence for RAMs: Li and Tell record Williams's observation that quadratic simulation by one tape followed by a two-fifths-power space simulation gives about $t^{4/5}$ space up to logarithms. The derivation supplies no corresponding near-linear time bound. [PPST](https://trotter.math.gatech.edu/papers/34.pdf) · [Williams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17779) · [Strassle and Subercaseaux](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11832) · [Li and Tell, §1 footnote 1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/233/download/)

The decoded-snapshot and producer proofs give such speedups for two classes of random-access executions that can touch large memory. Extending their coverage to every fast solver remains the prerequisite for this route. A proof must certify that every imported word has the value of its last write and pay for this certification in the saved clock. A construction for a strict subclass should identify how an unrestricted solver could escape it. The hierarchy is one route to a contradiction; the gate also admits a different unconditional theorem with a proved transfer.

Ordinary SAT in the stated explicit encoding can guess an assignment and check the formula in linear word time. Excluding a deterministic linear-time solver would therefore also separate nondeterministic from deterministic linear time on this RAM. Quasilinear completeness in the opposite direction loses logarithmic factors. A lower bound for an arithmetic circuit or for permanent-graph decision trees likewise needs a separate, costed transfer to Boolean SAT. [SAT transfer and its logarithmic losses](https://lance.fortnow.com/papers/files/tst.pdf) · [Permanent-graph analysis](pr249-grid.md)

The additive query measure led to a different verification argument. Guess a single segment's adaptive read log and use a separate nonacceptance test for an incorrect decoded value. The cumulative sum of shortest description length plus query time now excludes every exponent below $1.3760858894\ldots$ unconditionally. Under linear SAT there is an interval of $\Omega(n/\log^8 n)$ times with additive query cost $\Omega(n/\log^{12} n)$ at every time. The same approach with an intervening-write test forces hard predictions even when only imminent incoming reads matter. A matching shortcut for arbitrary native computations is missing.
